1. The short version

PingDrop has no servers. There is no account to create, nothing to log in to, and no backend that your data passes through. Everything PingDrop reads is encrypted on the device that reads it and sent directly to your other device, over your own Wi-Fi network or a direct Bluetooth link. The developer cannot see any of it, because none of it is ever sent anywhere the developer controls.

The exceptions are the services of Google that the Android app uses — advertising in the free version, Google Play Billing for purchases, and Google Play for update checks — each described below. The Mac app makes no internet connections at all.

2. What the phone app reads, and where it goes

Notification content

With your permission (granted on Android's Notification Access screen), PingDrop reads notifications posted on your phone: the app name, title, text, and Android's internal notification key.

Where it goes: encrypted and sent directly to your paired Mac, where it is shown as a normal macOS notification. When a notification is removed on one device it is removed on the other, if you have 2-way sync turned on — only the notification's key is sent for that.

Where it does not go: anywhere else. It is not sent to any server, not written to any log in release builds, and not kept by the app once delivered. You choose which apps are mirrored, and notification sync can be switched off entirely.

Clipboard content

Mac → phone is automatic when enabled: text you copy on your Mac is encrypted and placed on your phone's clipboard. Phone → Mac only ever happens when you ask for it — the Quick Settings tile, the share sheet, or the button in the app. Android does not let apps read the clipboard in the background, and PingDrop does not try to.

PingDrop never sends clipboard content marked as sensitive. On the phone that is content flagged EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE; on the Mac it is content that password managers mark as concealed or transient. In practice, passwords copied from a password manager are not synced, by design.

Where it goes: encrypted, directly to your paired device. Nowhere else.

Files you choose to send

Only files you pick — in the app's file picker, from the Android share sheet, or by dropping them onto PingDrop on your Mac. PingDrop never scans your storage and never sends anything you did not choose.

Where it goes: encrypted, over a direct connection to your paired device on your local network. Received files are saved to your Downloads folder, or to a folder you choose (PingDrop Pro).

Your installed app list

To show the right icon next to a mirrored notification, PingDrop sends your paired Mac the name and icon of apps that post notifications, and each app's last-update time so icons refresh when an app updates. The icons are cached on your Mac. This list never leaves your local network.

Battery level and device name

Your phone's charge level, charging state and device name (manufacturer and model) are sent to your paired Mac so it can show them; the Mac sends its own back. Local only.

A random device identifier

The first time PingDrop runs, the phone app creates a random identifier for itself. It is sent, encrypted, only to your paired Mac, so the Mac can tell your phone apart if a second phone scans the same pairing code — a pairing belongs to the one phone that claimed it. It is not linked to you, your Google account or any hardware identifier, and it is never sent anywhere else.

App versions

The phone and Mac apps tell each other which version they are, so either can tell you when the other needs an update.

3. What the Mac app reads, and where it goes

The Mac app makes no internet connections. Everything below stays on your Mac, or goes encrypted only to your paired phone.

  • Your clipboard — only when Mac → phone clipboard sync is on, to send what you copy to your phone. Concealed and transient content (passwords) is skipped.
  • Your Mac's battery level — sent to your phone so it can show it.
  • Your calendars — only if you turn on calendar reminders (PingDrop Pro) and allow access when macOS asks. See below.
  • Files you drop onto PingDrop, and files received from your phone, saved in Downloads or a folder you choose.
  • Crash reports — if PingDrop crashes or freezes, macOS hands it a diagnostic report (stack traces and system details, never your messages, clipboard or files). Reports are kept on your Mac and never sent automatically. You can open them from PingDrop's Preferences to attach to an email if you want to report a problem, or delete them there.

4. PingDrop Pro features

These are only active for PingDrop Pro users, and most can be switched off individually. None of them involves a server: like everything else, the data moves only between your two devices.

Reply from your Mac PRO

For notifications from apps that support replying from the notification (such as WhatsApp, Telegram and Messages), the Mac shows a Reply box. The text you type is encrypted, sent to your phone, and handed to that app through Android's standard reply mechanism — the same one smartwatches use. PingDrop does not keep a copy of it.

Images on the clipboard PRO

Images and screenshots you copy on your Mac are placed on your phone's clipboard, like text. An image goes from your phone to your Mac only when you send it — Push clipboard to Mac, the Quick Settings tile, or the share sheet. Images travel over Wi-Fi only. They are compressed for the transfer and the most recent one is kept in PingDrop's private storage on the phone so it can be pasted. Images are not added to the clipboard history.

Calendar reminders PRO

If you turn this on in PingDrop's Preferences on your Mac and allow calendar access when macOS asks, the Mac reads your upcoming events and sends each one to your phone shortly before it starts: its title, start and end time, location and calendar name. All-day events are skipped. Your calendar is read only on your Mac and sent, encrypted, only to your paired phone. You can turn it off on either device, and withdraw calendar access at any time in macOS System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars.

Notification rules, battery alerts, file settings PRO

Quiet hours, which apps are quiet or priority, battery alert levels, auto-accepting files and the folder files are saved to are settings stored on your own devices. A folder you choose is accessed only through the permission you grant in the system folder picker.

Backups. On Android, the pairing key, clipboard history and transfer history are excluded from Google cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer, so they cannot follow you to another phone without your knowledge. 6. How it is protected

  • Everything sent between your devices is encrypted with AES-256-GCM, using a key created on your Mac and shared only by scanning its QR code with your phone.
  • Every message carries a fresh random value and a timestamp, and old or repeated messages are refused, so recorded traffic cannot be played back later.
  • A pairing belongs to one phone. A second phone that scans the same code is turned away.
  • Received file names are sanitised so they can only ever be saved into the destination folder.

7. Advertising (free version of the Android app only)

The free version of the Android app shows banner advertisements served by Google AdMob. To serve ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects information including your device's advertising ID, device and network information, and IP address. This data is collected by Google, not by PingDrop's developer, and is governed by Google's policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy: policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from apps that use its services: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in Settings → Google → Ads (or Settings → Privacy → Ads) on your phone.

PingDrop Pro removes advertising entirely. When Pro is active, no ad is loaded and no ad request is made. The ads never receive your notifications, clipboard or files.

8. Purchases and updates

Purchases. PingDrop Pro is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The developer never sees or stores your payment details. Your purchase is Google Play's record, which is why it restores automatically on a new device or after a reinstall, with no account and no login.

Update checks. The Android app asks Google Play whether a newer version is available, using Google Play's in-app update service. This exchange is between your phone and Google Play; no information about you is sent to the developer.

Links. Links in the apps — Buy me a coffee, the GitHub page, the Play Store listing, this policy — open in your browser or the Play Store. Those sites have their own privacy policies.

9. What PingDrop does not do

  • No analytics, tracking or third-party crash-reporting SDK
  • No developer-operated server; nothing is transmitted to the developer
  • No account, sign-in, email collection or user profile
  • No selling or sharing of personal data with third parties, other than the advertising described above
  • No background reading of the phone's clipboard
  • No scanning of your files or storage
  • No location tracking (see the Bluetooth note below)

11. Deleting your data

All PingDrop data lives on your own devices, so you are in control of all of it:

  • Unpair: Disconnect in the phone app, or Pair New Phone on the Mac, deletes the pairing key and stops all syncing.
  • Clear history: the clipboard and transfer history screens on both devices have a clear action.
  • Crash reports: PingDrop's Preferences on the Mac → Diagnostics → Delete.
  • Calendar access: turn calendar reminders off, or withdraw access in macOS System Settings → Privacy & Security → Calendars.
  • Remove everything: uninstalling PingDrop deletes all of its data from your phone. On the Mac, delete the app; its data is removed with its app container.

There is no server-side data to request the deletion of, because there is no server.
