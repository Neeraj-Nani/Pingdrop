PingDrop — Privacy Policy

Effective 21 August 2026 · Contact pingdrop.support@gmail.com

PingDrop is developed by Neeraj Reddy.

The short version

PingDrop has no servers. There is no account to create, nothing to log in to, and no

backend that your data passes through. Everything the app reads from your phone is

encrypted on the phone and sent directly to the Mac you paired with, over your own

Wi-Fi network or a direct Bluetooth link. The developer cannot see any of it,

because none of it is ever sent anywhere the developer controls.

The one exception is advertising, which is served by Google AdMob in the free version

and is described in full below.

What PingDrop reads, and where it goes

PingDrop needs access to some genuinely personal things in order to do its job.

Here is each one, why it is needed, and where it goes.

Notification content

With your permission (granted through Android's Notification Access screen), PingDrop

reads notifications posted on your phone — the app name, title, body text, and

Android's internal notification key.

Where it goes: encrypted with AES-256-GCM and sent directly to your paired Mac

over your local Wi-Fi network, or over Bluetooth Low Energy when Wi-Fi is

unavailable. It is displayed there as a native macOS notification.

Where it does not go: anywhere else. It is not sent to any server, not written to

any log in release builds, and not retained by the app after it has been delivered.

You control this per app. Notification sync can be switched off entirely, and

individual apps can be excluded.

Clipboard content

Mac → phone is automatic when enabled: text copied on your Mac is encrypted and

written to your phone's clipboard.

Phone → Mac only ever happens when you explicitly ask for it — the Quick Settings

tile, the share sheet, or the button in the app. PingDrop does not monitor your

clipboard in the background.

PingDrop refuses to transmit any clipboard content that the source application has

flagged as sensitive (EXTRA_IS_SENSITIVE). In practice this means passwords copied

from a password manager are not synced, by design.

Where it goes: encrypted, directly to your paired device. Nowhere else.

Files you choose to transfer

Only files you explicitly select — through the in-app picker, the Android share

sheet, or by dropping them onto PingDrop on your Mac. PingDrop never scans your

storage and never transfers anything you did not pick.

Where it goes: encrypted in 4 MB chunks over a direct connection to your paired

device on your local network. Received files are saved to your Downloads folder.

Your installed app list

To display the correct icon next to a mirrored notification, PingDrop sends your

paired Mac the name and launcher icon of apps that produce notifications, along with

each app's last-update timestamp so icons can be refreshed when an app updates.

Where it goes: encrypted, to your paired Mac only, where the icons are cached

locally. This list is never transmitted off your local network.

Battery level and device name

Your phone's charge level, charging state, and device name (manufacturer and model)

are sent to your paired Mac so it can show them. The Mac sends its own back. Local

only.

Your pairing key

When you scan the QR code on your Mac, your phone stores the AES-256 key that

encrypts everything above.

This key is stored only in your phone's private app storage, and is **deliberately

excluded from Google cloud backup and from device-to-device transfer**, so it cannot

follow you onto another handset without your knowledge. Re-pairing is a QR scan, so

nothing is lost by not restoring it.

Transfer history

A record of your file transfers — filename, size, direction, outcome, timestamp — is

kept in a capped list of the 100 most recent, in the app's private storage on your

device. You can clear it at any time from the History screen. It never leaves the

device.

Advertising (free version only)

The free version of PingDrop shows banner advertisements served by Google AdMob.

To serve ads, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects information including your device's

advertising ID, device and network information, and IP address. This data is

collected by Google, not by PingDrop's developer, and is governed by Google's own

policies:

  • Google Privacy Policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy
  • How Google uses information from partner sites and apps:

https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time in

Settings → Google → Ads on your device.

Purchasing PingDrop Pro removes advertising entirely. When Pro is active, no ad

view is created and no ad request is ever made.

Purchases

PingDrop Pro is a one-time purchase handled entirely by Google Play Billing. The

developer never sees or stores your payment details. Your entitlement is Google Play's

record of your purchase — which is why it restores automatically on a new device or

reinstall with no account and no login.

What PingDrop does not do

  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDK of any kind
  • No developer-operated server; no data is transmitted to the developer
  • No account, sign-in, email collection, or user profile
  • No selling or sharing of personal data with third parties, other than the

advertising described above

  • No background clipboard monitoring
  • No scanning of your files or storage

Children

PingDrop is not directed at children under 13 and is not designed for their use.

Data deletion

All PingDrop data lives on your own devices. To remove it:

  • Unpair: Disconnect in the app deletes the pairing key and stops all syncing.
  • Clear history: the History screen has a clear action.
  • Remove everything: uninstalling PingDrop deletes all of its data from your

phone. Delete the macOS app to remove its cached icons and history.

There is no server-side data to request the deletion of, because there is no server.
